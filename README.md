## 📖 About This Project

The **Hunger Management Model** is an educational framework developed by **Dr. Bruce Thompson**, author of:

> **_Silent Killers No More: The Modern Health Crisis Hiding in Plain Sight_**

This model reframes hunger not as a willpower problem, but as a **signal-processing system** — one that can be distorted, hijacked, and reclaimed. It is designed to help individuals, clinicians, educators, and health advocates understand the true drivers of modern eating behavior and take evidence-informed action.

---

## 🎯 Purpose

Most weight and eating interventions fail because they treat hunger as a simple, uniform biological experience. The Hunger Management Model challenges that assumption by mapping the **three domains** that generate hunger signals and introducing a **phenotype-based framework** that explains why different people experience hunger so differently — and why one-size-fits-all approaches rarely work.

The goal is to change the narrative:

| Old Story | New Story |
|---|---|
| "I lack discipline" | "My signals were hijacked" |
| "I just need to eat less" | "I need to understand my hunger type" |
| "This is a willpower problem" | "This is a signal-processing problem" |

## 🔬 The Three Domains of Hunger

Hunger does not arise from a single source. This model maps it across **three interacting domains**:

### 1. 🧬 Biological Signals
Evolution-shaped hormonal and neurological signals designed to regulate energy balance and survival.

| Signal | Role |
|---|---|
| **Ghrelin** | Rises before meals; pulses tied to circadian rhythm |
| **GLP-1 / PYY / CCK** | Post-meal satiety hormones that signal fullness to the brain |
| **Leptin & Insulin** | Reflect long-term energy stores and fat mass |
| **Hepatic Glycogen** | Liver glycogen status communicates short-term fuel availability |
| **Autonomic Tone** | Sympathetic vs. parasympathetic balance regulates digestion speed |

**Normal Cycle:** `Hunger → Eating → Satiety → Quiet Interval → (repeat)`

### 2. 🎯 Cue-Driven Signals
Conditioned environmental, social, and psychological triggers that activate eating behavior **independent of true biological need**.

- Visual food cues (advertising, packaging, food placement)
- Emotional states (stress, boredom, reward-seeking)
- Social norms and meal timing pressures
- Habitual associations (screen time + snacking)

### 3. 🏗️ Structural Forces
Systemic and environmental factors that shape food availability, access, and consumption at scale.

- Ultra-processed food design (hyper-palatability engineering)
- Food marketing targeting vulnerable populations
- Food deserts and socioeconomic access gaps
- Policy and agricultural subsidies that favor cheap, calorie-dense products

## 🧩 The Four Hunger Phenotypes

Not all hunger is the same. The model defines **four distinct phenotypes** based on how accurately a person's hunger signals reflect their actual biological needs:

| Phenotype | Description |
|---|---|
| **Accurate** | Hunger signals closely match true energy needs; eating is largely self-regulating |
| **Distorted** | Signals are present but misread — often from dieting history or poor interoceptive awareness |
| **Hijacked** | External cues and engineered foods override biological signaling |
| **Collapsed** | Chronic dysregulation; signals are blunted, chaotic, or absent |

> Identifying your phenotype is the first step toward a targeted intervention strategy.

---

## 🏥 Intervention Tiers

The model uses a **progressive, tiered approach** — each tier builds on the one below it and is matched to phenotype severity:

```
Tier 1 — Lifestyle
        Circadian alignment, sleep, stress management, whole food patterns

Tier 2 — Behavioral
        Cue identification, hunger journaling, mindful eating, structured meals

Tier 3 — Medical
        GLP-1 receptor agonists, metabolic testing, clinical nutrition support

Tier 4 — Surgical
        Bariatric intervention for severe metabolic dysregulation
```
